### 3.2 The critical finding: post-$T_4$ recording margin is systematically short

For the 22 train events with an observable $T_4$, this phase computed **$T_4$ margin** = (back-camera stream duration) − (claimed $T_4$ value) — an objective, independently-verifiable arithmetic quantity, not a judgment call:

| $T_4$ margin | Event count |
|---|---:|
| < 1.5s | 10 |
| 1.5–3.0s | 10 |
| ≥ 3.0s | **2** (`event_011`: 7.97s; `event_019`: 3.93s) |

**20 of the 22 observable-$T_4$ train events (87%) have less than 3.0 seconds of recorded footage remaining after the ground-truth tail-clearance moment.** This is a property of the recording design itself (cameras were evidently stopped shortly after visual clearance, not held running for a standard post-clearance interval), **independent of any model or decision-policy behavior** — it is not a consequence of anything found in Phases 8–10.1, though it is strongly consistent with and now gives a precise, ground-truth-side explanation for the specific engine-replay margin problem Phase 10.1 found in two of the eleven R04-validated events (`event_002`: 0.8s margin between the engine's own departure confirmation and the end of its cached evidence; `event_006`: 2.8s margin) — this phase shows that short post-clearance recording margin is not an anomaly affecting a couple of events, but the dominant pattern across the entire 23-event cohort, visible directly in the ground-truth annotations before any model is even involved.

**This must not be automatically labeled a model failure.** A system that reacts anywhere close to the frozen policy's designed 3.0-second clearance dwell (by design, a deliberate conservative safety margin, not a defect) cannot, in most of these recordings, be observed completing that dwell and reopening the gate within the available footage — the recording ends first. Any future evaluation of clearance-latency-style metrics must distinguish this **right-censoring of the observation window** from a **genuine missed or late transition**, and must not conflate "the recording ended before we could see what the system would have done" with "the system failed to act."

## 6. The evaluation protocol (Task 6)

**Protocol version:** 1.0.0. **Defined before, and independent of, examining any model or decision-policy score** — every threshold below is derived either from the frozen system's own pre-existing, already-fixed timing constants (`configs/frozen_experiment_v1.yaml`, unchanged since before this phase) or from the ground-truth recording properties audited in Sections 2–3, never from observing what value would make any result look more favorable.

### 6.1 Eligible events per metric

As tabulated in Section 5. No metric may be computed, in any future phase, against an event outside its listed eligible set without an explicit, disclosed exception and rationale.

### 6.2 Treatment of censored and ambiguous events

Per the four-state taxonomy in `docs/PHASE11_GROUND_TRUTH_PROTOCOL.md` Section 2:
- **Censored** events/boundaries are excluded from the relevant metric's primary denominator. An exploratory, clearly-labeled lower/upper-bound figure may be reported separately (e.g. the existing protocol's left-censored-$T_0$ lower-bound exception), but must never be pooled into the primary statistic.
- **Uncertain** (ambiguity-flagged) boundaries may be included in a primary statistic only after genuine human review resolves or formally accepts the ambiguity (i.e., not from an unreviewed AI proposal); until then they are treated as not yet evaluable, not silently included.
- **Unobservable — Not Applicable** boundaries never count against any denominator (e.g. no-train events are never penalized for lacking a $T_0$).

### 6.3 Timing tolerance

**Tolerance $= 2 \times \text{tick\_s} = 2 \times 0.2\text{s} = 0.4\text{s}$**, using `configs/frozen_experiment_v1.yaml`'s existing `timing.tick_s` (vision sampling interval, 0.2s) and `timing.confirm_frames` (2) — both fixed since before this phase and unrelated to any score. Rationale: the frozen system cannot register or confirm any state transition at a finer resolution than two consecutive sampling ticks; a tolerance tighter than this would be comparing the system against a resolution it is structurally incapable of achieving, regardless of annotation precision (which, at native 30–60fps, is far finer than 0.4s and is not the limiting factor). This is a **system-resolution-derived** tolerance, not a tuned one, and applies symmetrically (a system transition within $\pm 0.4\text{s}$ of the matched ground-truth boundary counts as on-time; outside it, as late/early by the measured difference).

### 6.4 Event matching

- **Train events and the engine-only event:** one ground-truth passage interval per recording, matched 1:1 to at most one system-produced approach→clearance cycle within that recording. No cross-event matching is needed (each recording contains exactly zero or one train passage by construction).
- **No-train events:** no ground-truth passage exists to match against. Instead, define a **nuisance episode** as a maximal contiguous interval of system time where the decision-engine state is in `{WARNING, GATE_CLOSING, TRAIN_PRESENT, CROSSING_OCCUPIED, TRAIN_DEPARTING, REAR_CONFIRMED, CROSSING_CLEAR, GATE_OPENING}` (i.e., any non-`IDLE`, non-`FAULT_FAIL_SAFE` active state), bounded by returns to `IDLE`. Each such episode counts as one nuisance event; internal sub-transitions within a single unbroken episode are not separately counted.

### 6.5 False-positive and missed-event definitions

- **Missed event** (train/engine events only): the system never reaches an active approach-detecting state (`WARNING` or beyond) within [$T_1$ − tolerance, $T_4$ + tolerance] of the matched ground-truth interval. Requires $T_1$ observable (Section 5); events where $T_1$ is censored are excluded from this determination, not scored as either a hit or a miss.
- **False positive** (no-train events only): any nuisance episode (Section 6.4) occurring anywhere in a `no_train_events` recording. Each no-train event's exposure duration (its own recorded length; see Section 2) is the denominator for a rate, not a fixed assumed duration.
- **Premature opening** is tracked separately from "missed event" (Section 6.6) — a different failure mode (early release vs. no response at all) and must never be conflated with it in reporting.

### 6.6 Premature opening, warning lead-time, and clearance-latency calculations

- **POR** (unchanged from the existing protocol, `docs/FINAL_REAL_WORLD_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md` §18): gate opening commanded while $t < T_4$ (and $t \ge T_1$), restricted to the 22/23 $T_4$-observable events (Section 5).
- **Warning Lead Time** ($T_1 - T_{\text{detect}}$): computed only for the 22/23 $T_1$-observable events, where $T_{\text{detect}}$ (the system's own first qualifying-approach tick) is matched within the event's recorded window; reported with the $\pm 0.4$s tolerance band (Section 6.3) applied to the matching, not to the reported value itself.
- **Clearance Latency** ($T_{\text{open}} - T_4$): per Section 5, a **primary, unqualified point estimate is restricted to the 2/23 events with $\ge 3.0$s of post-$T_4$ margin** (`event_011`, `event_019`). For the other 20 observable-$T_4$ events, only a right-censored statement is permitted ("gate had not yet reopened by the end of the available recording, $X.XX$s after $T_4$" — a lower bound on latency, never treated as the latency itself).

### 6.7 How uncertainty and missing observations are handled

- Any event with a missing camera stream, a failed checksum, or an unreadable file is excluded entirely from every metric requiring that stream (none of the current 27 events are in this state — confirmed in Section 2 — but the rule is stated for future cohort growth).
- Any metric's reported denominator must state exactly which events were included and excluded, and why (censored, not applicable, insufficient margin, or missing data) — an unexplained denominator is not an acceptable reporting format under this protocol.
- Confidence intervals (e.g. Wilson 95% CIs, per the existing protocol's statistical analysis plan) must be computed on the eligible-event count actually used, never on the full nominal cohort size, when any event was excluded.
